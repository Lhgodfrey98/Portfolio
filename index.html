About me

I grew up in Evansville, Indiana, and found my way to medicine through a combination of science, language, and a deep belief that healthcare should be accessible to everyone. Studying abroad in Chile and Spain shaped how I see patients — not just as cases, but as whole people navigating complex systems in a language that may not be their own.

I earned dual bachelor's degrees in Chemistry and Spanish from IUPUI, where I was a Bepko Scholar and graduated with highest honors. I then completed my Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine at Marian University Wood College of Osteopathic Medicine in Indianapolis, IN, graduating in December 2025, where I was selected as the Graduate Commencement Speaker for Marian University's Class of 2026.

At AACOM, I work at the intersection of medical education, student outreach, and physician recruitment, creating and supporting projects like the national DO Shadowing Network and educational resource initiatives that help connect prospective students to osteopathic medicine on a national level.

In July 2026, I begin my internal medicine residency at the University of Kentucky, where I look forward to combining clinical depth with my ongoing commitment to medical education, health equity, and working with diverse and underserved populations.

✅ Reality

"DO school is easier to get into than MD school."
✅ Reality

❌ Common Myth
"DOs and MDs have separate residency tracks."
✅ Reality

✅ Reality

"A DO degree is less prestigious than an MD."
✅ Reality
Both degrees confer full physician licensure in the U.S. DOs hold leadership positions across academic medicine, military medicine, and major hospital systems.

✅ Reality
Many strong applicants strategically choose DO programs for their philosophy, primary care orientation, or geographic fit. DO and MD are parallel paths, not a hierarchy.

Advisor Scenario Response Guide

"I got rejected from MD schools — should I apply DO?" ▾
Reframe this as a genuine choice, not a fallback. Explore whether they've shadowed a DO, understand the philosophy, and can speak to why osteopathic medicine specifically interests them. If yes, DO is a strong fit. If not, help them develop that narrative first.
"My GPA is 3.5 and MCAT is 504 — am I competitive for DO?" ▾
Yes — this is within the competitive range for many DO programs. The average DO matriculant GPA is ~3.62 and MCAT ~503. Emphasize that holistic review means their clinical experience, service, and commitment also matter significantly.
"I don't want to just do primary care." ▾
Good news — DOs match into every specialty. 43% of DO graduates practice in non-primary care specialties including emergency medicine, surgery, and anesthesiology. Share this clearly and point them to specialty-specific data.
"Will a DO degree limit my options compared to an MD?" ▾
No. Since the 2020 merger, DOs and MDs compete in the same residency match and train in the same programs. The degree type has no bearing on specialty access or hospital privileges.
"My parents think DO is less prestigious." ▾
Acknowledge the concern, then provide data: DOs serve as military physicians, lead major hospital systems, and practice in every specialty. The 2020 merger means the residency landscape is identical. Help the student have this conversation with their family.

Why Campus DO Events Matter

  • Students who meet a DO physician are far more likely to seriously consider the DO path — and to shadow one afterward.
  • Panels, Q&As, and classroom visits normalize osteopathic medicine as a career option alongside MD programs.
  • A single well-connected DO speaker can open doors to shadowing opportunities for your entire student cohort.
  • For students in the Exploration stage, a DO speaker event is often the experience that moves them to commitment.

Step-by-Step: Hosting a DO Event

  1. Decide on your format: single speaker, panel, DO medical student visit, or virtual event.
  2. Identify your audience: open pre-med event, pre-health club meeting, specific class.
  3. Find a speaker using the AOA state chapter, nearest DO school outreach office, or AACOM speaker request.
  4. Reach out 4–6 weeks in advance with a formal invitation including purpose, audience size, duration, and logistics.
  5. Prepare students before the event: share background and ask them to prepare 2–3 questions.
  6. After the event, collect contact info from any physician willing to be a future shadowing contact.
  7. Send a thank-you note within 48 hours.
  8. Document the event: speaker name, specialty, date, student attendance, and follow-up commitments.
Sample Email Subject: "Invitation to Speak with Pre-Med Students — [Your University]"

Include: your role, brief description of the student audience, proposed 30–60 minute format (in person or virtual), and an offer to handle all logistics. Always follow up with a formal thank-you within 48 hours.

In Development

DO Shadow Network

A nationwide platform connecting pre-medical students with osteopathic physicians willing to host shadowing experiences — built to close one of the most consistent gaps in the DO pipeline.

The problem: Pre-med students across the country consistently report they don't have access to DOs to shadow. Shadowing a DO is one of the strongest drivers of students choosing to apply to osteopathic medical school — not lower academics, but meaningful, personal exposure to osteopathic medicine. The DO Shadow Network is designed to fix that pipeline gap at scale.
🔍
Student Search
Students search by state and specialty — "I'm in Indiana and want to shadow a DO in family medicine" — and receive matched physician profiles from the database.
🩺
Physician Opt-In
DOs voluntarily register as available for shadowing. Participation is clearly framed as separate from clinical precepting — one does not replace the other.
🤝
State AOA Partnership
State Osteopathic Medical Associations hold DO membership and relationships. They are the primary channel for recruiting participating physicians in each state.
📋
Accountability System
Built-in no-show tracking protects physician time. Two missed appointments without notice triggers account deactivation and a formal notification to the student.

What the Platform Will Look Like

Student-Facing Features

  • Search by state, specialty, and availability
  • View physician profiles with practice setting and contact info
  • Request shadowing appointments through the platform
  • Confirmation and reminder notifications
  • Post-shadowing reflection prompts and logging
  • Account deactivated after 2 no-shows without notice

Physician-Facing Features

  • Simple opt-in registration — specialty, location, availability windows
  • Approve or decline shadowing requests
  • Set capacity limits (e.g., 1 student per month)
  • No-show reporting with automatic escalation
  • Clear messaging: shadowing ≠ precepting
  • State AOA-verified profiles for credibility

Phased Rollout Plan

PhaseFocusKey Actions
Phase 1 — Pilot2–3 state AOA partners, limited specialtiesRecruit early-adopter DOs via state AOA relationships; test platform UX; gather student and physician feedback
Phase 2 — Expand10–15 states, broader specialty coverageUse pilot data to refine onboarding; scale state AOA outreach; launch advisor-facing portal for prehealth offices
Phase 3 — NationalAll 50 states, full specialty coverageFull public launch; integrate with AACOM's existing physician locator; annual DO recruitment campaigns

Messaging to DOs

  • This is not precepting. Shadowing is observation only — no clinical responsibility, no supervision paperwork.
  • You set the terms. You control when, how often, and how many students you host.
  • Your time is protected. The platform screens students and enforces no-show accountability so your schedule is respected.
  • You shape the future of your profession. A single shadowing experience can be the reason a student chooses osteopathic medicine.

Messaging to Advisors & Students

  • Access where there was none. Students in any state can now find a DO willing to host them, regardless of geography or specialty interest.
  • A genuine connection. Shadowing through the network is a meaningful experience — not just a box to check. Reflection tools help students articulate what they observed.
  • Respect the physicians' time. Students who no-show twice are deactivated. The platform only works if both sides hold up their end.
Current status: The DO Shadow Network is in active development. State AOA partnership strategy is underway. If you have connections to DOs interested in early participation or state associations ready to pilot, contact the AACOM UME team at UMETeam@aacom.org.

The Pre-Med Journey & The DO Pathway

A real-world perspective from medical school to residency — presented by Laurinda Godfrey, DO (Marian University Wood COM, Class of Dec. 2025).

About the Presenter

Laurinda Godfrey, DO

Desired Specialty: Internal Medicine → Pulm/Critical Care
Medical School: Marian University Wood COM, Indianapolis, IN

"I chose DO because it's a community rooted in humility, inclusivity, and an unwavering compassion for people."

Session Goals

  • Talk honestly about the pre-med and medical school journey
  • Normalize challenges, detours, and self-doubt
  • Explore the DO pathway with clarity, not assumptions
  • Focus on wellness and sustainability, not just achievement
  • Reassure that uncertainty does not mean you don't belong
Slide 5–7
Understanding the Pre-Med Journey
The pre-med journey requires dedication, strategic planning, and a commitment to gaining diverse experiences. Key stages: undergraduate studies → MCAT → application → medical school → residency. Uncertainty is normal. Comparison can be constant — but it doesn't have to be.
Slide 8–10
Discovering the DO Pathway
Osteopathic medicine emphasizes a holistic approach, focusing on preventive care and the body's natural ability to heal. DOs are not limited in scope of practice — the field spans every specialty from family medicine and pediatrics to geriatrics and surgery.
Slide 12
Professionalism in Medicine
Integrity & Ethics — Upholding ethical standards builds trust and credibility.
Respect & Compassion — Kindness fosters a supportive environment for patients and colleagues.
Accountability — Taking ownership builds a solid reputation.
Continuous Improvement — Seeking feedback enhances both development and patient care.
Slide 13–14
Sustainability & Wellness
School-Life Balance — Promotes well-being and leads to better patient care.
Support Systems — Mentorship and peer networks provide guidance through challenges.
Self-Care & Mindfulness — Regular self-care promotes overall well-being and mental clarity.
Ongoing Education — Adapting to the evolving field through classes and rotations.
Slide 25
Building Your Application
Strong DO applications highlight: shadowing with DO physicians (50–100 hours), leadership experience, community service, meaningful clinical exposure, and a personal statement that speaks specifically to osteopathic philosophy — not just medicine in general.
Key Takeaways
Your Pathway to a Successful Career
The DO pathway offers clinical excellence, whole-person care, and access to every medical specialty. Prioritize wellness from the start. Build genuine mentorship relationships. Let the osteopathic philosophy — not just the degree — guide your approach to practice.
